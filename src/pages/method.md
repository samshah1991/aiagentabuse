This page explains how an incident gets onto the site, and how we fix it when we get something wrong.

## Inclusion criteria

An incident is included when all of these are true:

- An **AI agent** was involved: a system that uses a model to plan and take actions through tools, not only generate text.
- Real harm, or a credible demonstrated risk, occurred outside a lab, or was disclosed by the vendor or a researcher with evidence.
- At least one **public, citable source** exists.
- It fits exactly one **track**: the agent caused the harm (`cause`), or an attacker manipulated the agent (`target`).

## Sourcing

- We prefer primary sources: vendor post-mortems, security advisories, court records, and first-hand accounts from the affected party.
- Secondary reporting is accepted when it cites its own evidence. A single social media post is not enough on its own.
- Each source is listed with its title, URL, and the date we accessed it.
- Summaries are written **in our own words**. We do not reproduce long quotations.
- We do not publish exploit code, working attack payloads, or unpatched vulnerability details.

## Verification

Every entry has a `verification_status`:

- **draft**: proposed, not yet checked. Drafts are clearly labelled and should not be cited.
- **verified**: a human reviewer has checked the claims against the cited sources.
- **corrected**: verified, then changed after publication. See below.

Nothing generated by an automated agent is marked verified without human review.

## Dating

- `date` is the date the incident happened. If that is unknown, we use the date it was first publicly reported, and say so in the summary.
- `last_reviewed` is the date a human last checked the entry against its sources.
- All dates use the `YYYY-MM-DD` format.

## Corrections {#corrections}

- Anyone can request a correction through the [submission form](/submit/) or a pull request.
- Material corrections change the status to **corrected**, update `last_reviewed`, and are recorded in the repository history, which is public.
- Vendors and named parties may send a right-of-reply with evidence; we review it the same way as any other source.
- If an entry cannot be supported by sources, we remove it and note the removal in the commit history.

## Evidence scoring {#evidence}

Defense checklists may show an **evidence grade**. An automated agent searches for research and benchmarks and extracts findings. A fixed formula then scores them, and **a human approves every update by merging a pull request**.

- Each finding is scored 0-100 from source type, study design, recency, and whether the authors sell the defense.
- A category score averages its best three independent sources, and is reduced when fewer than three independent sources exist.
- The score measures how strong the evidence is, **not** whether the defense works. Findings that contradict a defense are counted and shown separately.
- Every quote is checked by code against the fetched page. Findings that fail the check are discarded.

The formula is in `agent/score.mjs` and described in `docs/evidence-method.md`.
